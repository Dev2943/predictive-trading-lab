# ADR-0003 — A conservative fill model, not queue-position reconstruction

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-22
**Context:** The Phase 3 specification listed "queue position" under the execution simulator.
**Related:** ADR-0001 (honest project claim), Phase 1 review finding on CI policy guards

---

## The conflict

The Phase 3 brief asked for queue-position modelling. That request conflicts
directly with an already-Accepted ADR and with an enforced build guard:

- **ADR-0001** states: *"Do not claim queue-position realism at T2 or T3.
  Sampled top-of-book data cannot establish queue position, full displayed
  depth, cancellations, or intra-interval quote dynamics."*
- **`.github/workflows/ci.yml`** fails the build on any `queue_position` or
  `queue_pos` symbol appearing in `include/`, `src/` or `apps/`. That guard has
  been negative-tested: introducing the symbol does fail the job.

Implementing it silently would have produced a system whose honest-claim
statement was false and whose CI was red. Skipping it silently would have left
a spec item unaddressed with no record of why.

## Why queue position is not obtainable here

Queue position is the number of shares ahead of yours at your price level. It
requires knowing, for every order at that level, the sequence in which orders
arrived and the sequence in which they were cancelled. That needs **order-level
(L3 / market-by-order)** data.

Our data tiers, per ADR-0001, are:

| Tier | Schema | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | `ohlcv-1m` | One aggregate per minute. No book at all. |
| T2 | `cbbo-1m` | Consolidated best bid/offer, **sampled** each minute. |
| T3 | `cbbo-1s` | The same, sampled each second. |

None carries order identity, arrival sequence, or cancellation events. A queue
model built on them would be a **number generated by an assumption, presented as
a measurement**. It would move execution results — probably favourably, since a
modeller who cannot observe the queue tends to assume a friendlier position than
reality — and nothing downstream could detect the error.

## Decision

1. **No queue-position modelling, and no symbol by that name.** The CI guard
   stands.

2. **A conservative fill model instead** (`ptl::execution::FillConfig`), whose
   assumptions are enumerated and configurable:

   | Assumption | Default | Rationale |
   |---|---|---|
   | Marketable orders cross the spread | always | A taker pays the touch. Observable from the quote. |
   | Fills capped by displayed size | on | We can see top-of-book size at the sample instant. |
   | Fills capped by participation rate | 5% | Bounds our share of observed interval volume. |
   | Zero interval volume ⇒ no fill | on | Zero-volume minutes are real; "no cap computed" must not mean "unlimited". |
   | Passive limit needs a trade-through | on | A bar whose low equals your limit says nothing about whether you traded. |
   | Passive fill ratio | 0.5 | A pessimistic haircut expressing *"we were somewhere in a queue we cannot observe"*. |

3. **`passive_fill_ratio` is named for what it is.** It is a haircut, not a
   queue estimate. The field name, its comment, and this ADR all say so, and no
   report may describe it otherwise.

4. **Stop and stop-limit orders are implemented** — those were also in the brief
   and carry no such conflict.

## Consequences

**Positive.** The honest-claim statement in ADR-0001 stays true. Execution
results are conservative by construction: where the model is uncertain it
declines to fill rather than assuming a favourable position. Every assumption
has a named configuration field, so a reviewer can find and change each one.

**Negative.** Passive-execution research is bounded. We cannot credibly compare
maker-side strategies or study queue-jumping, and any result involving resting
limit orders carries the `passive_fill_ratio` haircut as an explicit, and
admittedly crude, parameter. Report passive results with a sensitivity sweep
over that ratio, exactly as costs get a multiplier sweep.

**Deferred.** If L3 data is ever acquired, queue modelling becomes possible and
this ADR should be revisited. That is a separate module with its own honest-claim
statement, not a quiet upgrade of this one — and the CI guard should be relaxed
deliberately, in the same commit, with the claim updated.

## Verification

- `tests/leakage/test_execution.cpp` — *"a non-marketable limit does not fill on
  a touch"*, *"zero interval volume means no fill not an unconstrained one"*,
  *"fills are capped by the participation rate"*, *"fills are capped by
  displayed size"*.
- CI policy guard, negative-tested: introducing a `queue_position` symbol fails
  the build.
